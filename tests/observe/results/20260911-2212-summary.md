## Triage of the 18 divergent ticks

1. **Tick 76 (40 divergences) — HARNESS-side read/apply race, NOT product
   divergence.** The tick's kubectl source read ran inside the 50%-batch's
   apply window: the burst goroutine had applied the pods (nacos already
   served 40 of them through the event path — the child registers within
   ~1s) but the ledger write lands only after `kubectl apply` returns, and
   the tick's source read had already completed pre-apply. 40 extras with
   no clock = DIVERGENT by the fail-safe rule. The next tick (77) was
   CONSISTENT at 1264/1264. Harness fix (recorded, not silently changed):
   write the ledger BEFORE the apply's return, or quiesce the tick during
   a burst apply.
2. **The burst delete-leg late-heal — a REAL product race, with the
   coalescing queue EXONERATED.** The mechanism, verified line-by-line
   from the child log (per-pod sequences in
   evidence/20260911-2212-burst-delete-race-perpod.log.txt): the
   re-register is NOT a late queued UPDATE racing past the delete — there
   is NO robot event for the cited pod after its delete (pod-1490's last
   events are two termination updates at 22:21:02.539/.545 and the
   delete at 22:21:02.553; those pre-delete updates were termination
   updates that produced the deregister itself, 22:21:02.596). The
   re-register is the 60s FULL-PUSH TICK'S PUSH WAVE: the tick's source
   snapshot was read BEFORE the burst delete events arrived — the
   22:21:02.304 tick snapshotted 1176 pods at 22:21:02.314 while the
   delete events started at 22:21:02.5 — and its bounded-parallel
   register wave (population-scale: ~340 nacos registers streamed
   22:21:02.45-.95) landed AFTER the event-path deregister completed
   (pod-1490: deregister 22:21:02.596, the wave's re-register
   22:21:02.947). The coalescing queue behaved correctly and is NOT
   implicated. The healer is the NEXT 60s tick's CompareAndFlush case-3
   (k8s.go:510 "atlas server pre delete instance size" — instances in
   the discovery center but not in k8s; pod-1490 healed 22:22:02.413 by
   the 22:22:02.299 tick's case-3, evidenced by the servIns-shaped rsync
   with Label:null/Reversion:204801 immediately preceding). delete →
   healed therefore = 60-82s > OBS_BOUND (60s) on 3 of 4 delete legs
   (storm 83s, batch@25% 82s, batch@50% 67s; batch@75% 41s passed).
   This is the burst window's product finding: **the delete leg of a
   burst rollout is not bounded by OBS_BOUND when the full-push tick's
   pre-delete snapshot pushes a register wave that lands after the
   event-path deregister; the next tick's case-3 is the healer, and its
   cadence (P) equals the bound, so any phase miss breaches.**

   Why the 2h sustained window saw ZERO divergence from the same wave:
   NOT pod age (the wave re-registers every pod every 60s regardless of
   age) — the real reason is PHASE COMMENSURABILITY. The 20s churn
   cadence and the 60s wave are commensurate and phase-locked into
   non-overlap: the wave's snapshot+push window sits at :59.0-.59.2 of
   each minute while the churn's delete-event windows occupy :0x-:1x /
   :2x-:3x / :4x-:5x, so a wave snapshot is never pre-delete stale —
   plus event-stream width: the sustained streams are ~17 pods (5%/min
   of 1000 per 20s tick) while the burst streams are 200 pods whose
   delete events, spanning seconds, landed ON the wave ticks
   (22:21:02.5 vs the 22:21:02.304 tick).
3. **The 75%-batch up-leg straggler (1m5s) — the only UP-leg breach.** Two
